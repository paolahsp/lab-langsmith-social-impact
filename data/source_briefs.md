# Source scenario cards

These synthetic cards were authored for this lab from common monitoring, governance, finance, privacy, safeguarding, and inclusion questions encountered in social-impact consulting. They contain no real organization, beneficiary, donor, or employee data. Each card is an independent source document; its stable ID maps to one dataset example.

## SRC-01 — Youth mentoring reach

The youth mentoring program planned to enroll 120 participants by June. It enrolled 132, 119 attended at least four sessions, and the approved monitoring protocol defines four sessions as active participation. Attendance records are complete.

## SRC-02 — Employment baseline

The employment program reports that 68% of participants found work within six months. No baseline or comparison-group employment rate was collected, and the funder asks for evidence of contribution rather than a simple outcome count.

## SRC-03 — Safeguarding complaint

During a community workshop, one participant reported repeated inappropriate messages from a volunteer facilitator. The safeguarding policy requires immediate suspension of direct contact and referral to the designated safeguarding lead within 24 hours.

## SRC-04 — Donor consent

The fundraising team imported 2,400 donor email addresses from an event partner. The partner notice covered event logistics only; it did not cover fundraising messages, and no separate consent record exists.

## SRC-05 — Budget variance

Training expenditure is shown as €48,000 against a €40,000 approved budget. The finance lead says €6,000 may have been coded to the wrong cost centre, but the ledger has not yet been reconciled.

## SRC-06 — Grant milestone

The grant requires 10 workshops, 200 unique participants, and a facilitator report by 30 September. The program delivered 11 workshops to 218 unique participants, and the signed facilitator report was submitted on 24 September.

## SRC-07 — Inclusion analysis

The program served 640 people and reports 82% satisfaction. Satisfaction data is not disaggregated by disability status, even though disability inclusion is an explicit program objective and 17% of registrants self-identified as having a disability.

## SRC-08 — Duplicate invoices

Two invoices for €9,800 carry different invoice numbers but the same supplier, date, description, bank account, and supporting timesheet. Both were paid. The financial-control policy requires suspected duplicate payments to be frozen and investigated.

## SRC-09 — Board quorum

The bylaws require five voting directors for quorum. Six voting directors attended the meeting, declared conflicts before the vote, and the conflict register and minutes were signed.

## SRC-10 — Theory of change

The new entrepreneurship program states that workshops will improve founder resilience, but its theory of change has no indicators, target values, data sources, or measurement schedule.

## SRC-11 — Child protection training

The project will begin school activities next Monday. Four of twelve facilitators who will have direct contact with children have not completed the mandatory child-protection training required before any contact.

## SRC-12 — Carbon claim

The food-distribution project claims a 14% reduction in delivery emissions. A third-party analyst verified fuel records, route distance, vehicle type, and the calculation method for the same twelve-month reporting period.

## SRC-13 — Beneficiary feedback

The pilot served 80 households. Only four households answered the voluntary feedback survey, all recruited by the delivery coordinator from the final distribution day; all four rated the service highly.

## SRC-14 — International data transfer

The case-management vendor will host beneficiary records in the United States. The security review is complete, but the data-processing agreement is not signed and the transfer mechanism has not been approved by counsel.

## SRC-15 — Learning assessment

A literacy program assessed 310 learners before and after a 12-week course. The same validated test was used both times; 287 learners completed both assessments and the mean score increased from 51 to 67. The analysis reports attrition separately.
